Best App to Learn Mandarin in 2026: My Honest Picks After Testing 12+

By Elena | April 18, 2026

Mandarin is genuinely one of the hardest languages for English speakers to learn — the tones alone send most beginners into a spiral of confusion. I know because I'm a native Mandarin speaker, and I've watched it happen firsthand with my language exchange partners. Several of them came to our sessions having "studied Mandarin for months" with an app, only to produce tones that were... creative, let's say. The apps had kept them busy, but not always taught them correctly.

That experience pushed me to actually test the apps they were using — and a bunch of others. I evaluated each one with a specific question in mind: does this actually teach Mandarin, or does it just make you feel like you're learning?

Here's what I found.

Quick Answer: For most beginners, HelloChinese is the best app to learn Mandarin. It's built specifically for Chinese, handles tones properly, and has a genuinely solid free tier. If speaking and listening are your priority, Pimsleur is the better pick. For reading practice at intermediate level, nothing beats Du Chinese.

The Best Apps to Learn Mandarin, Reviewed

HelloChinese — Best Overall for Beginners

If you're starting from zero and want one app to anchor your learning, HelloChinese is where I'd send you. It was designed exclusively for Mandarin — not adapted from a Spanish or French template the way many language apps are — and that difference shows up in the details that actually matter.

The tone training in HelloChinese is legitimately good. It uses visual pitch diagrams alongside audio, which means you're not just mimicking a sound blindly — you're understanding the shape of the tone. One of my language partners told me this was the first time tones "made sense" to her after months of confusion with other apps. From my perspective as a native speaker, the audio quality is natural and the pronunciation models are accurate. That's not a given with Chinese-learning apps.

What you get for free is substantial: the full beginner course up to approximately HSK 2–3 level. The Premium plan (~$12/mo, or ~$70/year) unlocks advanced content, a characters course, and graded reading stories. The Premium+ plan (~$150/year) adds immersion lessons and unlimited graded stories.

Best for: Absolute beginners who want a structured, Chinese-specific learning path.

Pros:

  • Designed specifically for Mandarin (not a generic language app)
  • Genuine tone training with visual + audio feedback
  • Clean, well-paced progression
  • Generous free tier
  • Covers reading, writing, listening, and speaking

Cons:

  • Most learners will outgrow it around HSK 3–4 level (roughly 6–12 months of consistent use)
  • Speaking practice, while present, is limited compared to dedicated conversation apps
  • The gamification can feel slightly less polished than Duolingo's

Pimsleur — Best for Speaking and Pronunciation

Pimsleur is built on a completely different philosophy than every other app on this list. There's no screen to stare at — it's 30-minute audio lessons, and your only job is to listen and respond out loud. If you've ever muttered phrases to yourself on a walk or commute, Pimsleur was basically made for that.

The method works because it uses spaced repetition in the audio itself — you hear a word, then get prompted to recall it at increasing intervals across lessons. After testing the first few Mandarin levels, I noticed the pronunciation focus is serious. It's slower and more deliberate than real conversational pace, which is by design and actually helps build correct muscle memory.

My language partners who used Pimsleur alongside HelloChinese consistently had better speaking rhythm than those who used just one or the other. That said, Pimsleur teaches almost no characters — if reading and writing are important to you, you'll need to pair it with something else.

The price is higher than most apps (~$20/month for a single language), which puts some people off. They do offer a 7-day free trial, and the annual all-languages plan at ~$165/year is a much better deal if you're committed.

Best for: Auditory learners, commuters, and anyone whose primary goal is to actually hold a conversation.

Pros:

  • Genuinely strong pronunciation and tone development
  • Works hands-free (perfect for commutes, gym, etc.)
  • Spaced repetition built into the audio format

Cons:

  • Almost no character or reading instruction
  • Higher price point
  • 30-minute lesson format requires a sustained time block

Du Chinese — Best for Reading Practice

Once you've gotten past the beginner stage and want to actually read Chinese, Du Chinese is the app I'd point you toward. It's a graded reading app — meaning it has a library of short articles and stories written at specific HSK levels, from absolute beginner to advanced — and the reading experience is genuinely well designed.

Tap any word and its pinyin and definition appear instantly. You can toggle between simplified and traditional characters, turn pinyin on or off, and enable HSK color coding to see at a glance which words are in your target vocabulary tier. The audiobook mode lets you listen while you read, which helps with both comprehension and pronunciation.

I've seen one of my language partners go from struggling with HSK 3 texts to comfortably reading HSK 4–5 articles over about three months of consistent Du Chinese use. It doesn't teach you new grammar or vocabulary from scratch — you need to already have a foundation — but it's the best tool I've found for bridging the gap between "studied Mandarin" and "can actually read something."

The free tier gives you access to articles released in the last two weeks; older content requires a paid subscription (~$120/year).

Best for: Intermediate learners who already know basic grammar and want to build reading fluency.

Pros:

  • Beautiful, thoughtfully designed reading interface
  • In-context vocabulary lookup is genuinely seamless
  • Six difficulty levels with a wide range of content topics
  • Audiobook mode for reading + listening simultaneously

Cons:

  • Not a course — you need a foundation before it's useful
  • Doesn't teach speaking or character writing
  • Free access is limited to recent articles

Skritter — Best for Mastering Characters

Writing Chinese characters by hand might feel like an optional extra, but there's a strong argument for learning it: studies suggest that the physical process of writing reinforces character recognition far more effectively than just reading. Skritter is the best app I've found for this specific skill.

It uses a handwriting recognition system where you trace characters on your screen with your finger, and it corrects your stroke order and technique in real time. Combined with spaced repetition, it means you practice the characters you're weakest on most often. You can also import vocabulary lists from textbooks or HSK word lists, which is useful if you're following a structured curriculum.

Skritter is genuinely specialized — it won't teach you grammar or listening comprehension. It does one thing. But it does that one thing better than anything else available. At ~$15/month, it's not cheap for a single-skill app, but for serious learners who want to actually write Chinese, it's worth it.

Best for: Intermediate to advanced learners who want to master character writing and recognition.

Pros:

  • Handwriting recognition with stroke order correction
  • Spaced repetition optimized for characters
  • Supports simplified and traditional characters
  • Integrates with existing vocabulary lists and textbooks

Cons:

  • Expensive relative to its narrow focus
  • Only useful once you have a foundation — not for total beginners
  • Some users find the UI feels dated

Rocket Chinese — Best if You Want a Structured Course

Rocket Chinese takes a different approach from most of the apps here: it's a full course you work through sequentially, not a gamified daily-habit tool. The lessons combine interactive audio dialogues (you hear native speakers in real conversation scenarios) with grammar explanations, cultural notes, and reinforcement exercises.

I've recommended this to language partners who feel overwhelmed by piecing together multiple apps and want one cohesive learning path. The structured progression makes sense and the audio quality is some of the best available. It's more like a textbook that talks than a mobile game.

The pricing model is also different — you pay once for lifetime access (~$150 for Level 1, up to ~$450 for all three levels) rather than a monthly subscription. There are frequent discounts, especially around holidays, so it's worth checking before paying full price.

The main critique I'd give: the lessons are very English-heavy. There's a lot of explanation before you actually hear Mandarin, which can slow the pace if you learn best by immersion.

Best for: Learners who want one structured, comprehensive program and don't want to manage multiple apps.

Pros:

  • Comprehensive coverage of grammar, vocabulary, and culture
  • High-quality native-speaker audio
  • Lifetime access (one-time payment)
  • Well-organized sequential curriculum

Cons:

  • Heavy English explanation can slow immersion
  • Less engaging than gamified apps — requires self-discipline to work through
  • No live speaking practice or AI conversation features

Pleco — The Dictionary Every Mandarin Learner Needs

Pleco isn't a course app — it's a reference tool, and it's the single most universally recommended app in any serious Mandarin learning community. The base app is free and includes two comprehensive dictionaries (combined, over 200,000 entries), handwriting input, pinyin lookup, and camera-based OCR (point your phone at a Chinese sign or menu and it reads the characters).

You'll want Pleco from day one, not because it teaches you Mandarin, but because it removes friction from everything else you're doing. When you encounter a character you don't know in Du Chinese, in a textbook, or in real life, Pleco is how you look it up instantly.

The add-on bundles unlock flashcards, enhanced handwriting, audio pronunciations, and additional dictionaries. The Basic Bundle (~$30) is sufficient for most learners; the Professional Bundle (~$60) is for serious advanced students.

Best for: Everyone learning Mandarin, as a companion to your primary learning app.

Pros:

  • Industry-standard dictionary with over 200,000 entries
  • Camera OCR for real-world character lookup
  • Genuinely excellent free version
  • One-time payment (no subscription)

Cons:

  • Not a course — won't teach you Mandarin on its own
  • Interface has a slightly utilitarian feel
  • OCR requires paid add-on for some devices

Duolingo — Best for Building a Daily Habit (But Know Its Limits)

I'll be direct about Duolingo: it's better as a consistency tool than as a Chinese teacher. The gamification — streaks, XP, leaderboards — is genuinely effective at keeping you opening the app every day. And for Mandarin, it does cover about 1,000 characters and gets you to roughly HSK 3 level over a full course completion.

Where Duolingo falls short for Chinese specifically is the depth of tone instruction and grammar explanation. The grammar is learned almost entirely by osmosis — you're expected to figure out patterns from repeated exposure, which works reasonably well for European languages but is genuinely harder for Mandarin where tonal context is everything.

My honest recommendation: use Duolingo as your daily habit-builder while doing something more substantive alongside it. HelloChinese is better at actually teaching Mandarin. But if Duolingo's streak system is what gets you showing up every day, that consistency matters — so it earns its place in the stack. For a deep dive on Duolingo's features and pricing, see my full Duolingo review and Duolingo pricing guide.

Best for: Habit-building and casual learners who want low-pressure daily practice.

Pros:

  • Genuinely excellent for habit formation
  • Completely free core course
  • Wide variety of exercise formats
  • Covers all four skills

Cons:

  • Weak tone training compared to HelloChinese
  • Almost no grammar explanations
  • Gamification can distract from actual learning
  • Heart/mistake limit system in free tier can frustrate serious learners

How I Evaluated These Apps

My perspective here is different from most app reviewers: I'm a native Mandarin speaker who tested these tools and who has watched language exchange partners use them over time. That means I could evaluate them on dimensions that non-native reviewers might miss.

I assessed each app on five criteria:

1. Tone Training Quality — Does the app actually teach the four tones correctly, or does it just play audio and hope you pick it up? This is the make-or-break factor for Mandarin specifically.

2. Character Coverage — Does the app teach you to read characters, or does it keep you in Pinyin dependency? (Pinyin is a useful scaffold; it shouldn't become a crutch.)

3. Speaking Practice — Can you actually produce language, or are you only doing recognition exercises?

4. Progression & Structure — Is there a logical path from where you are to where you want to be, or is it just random vocabulary?

5. Value for Money — Does the free tier give you real learning value, or is it a teaser for a subscription wall?

The App Stack — What Serious Learners Actually Use

Here's something most "best apps" lists don't tell you: no single app covers everything. Mandarin has enough distinct skill components that the most effective learners combine tools.

Here's the stack I've seen work best, by level:

Beginner (0–6 months):

  • HelloChinese (main course) + Pleco (dictionary, from day one)
  • Optional: Duolingo if you need the streak system to build a habit

Intermediate (6–18 months):

  • Du Chinese (reading, 20–30 min/day) + Pleco (still essential)
  • Pimsleur or a conversation partner for speaking
  • Skritter if character writing is a priority

Advanced (18 months+):

  • Du Chinese at upper levels + native content (Chinese podcasts, TV shows)
  • Pleco for vocabulary mining
  • Live tutoring or conversation exchange to push fluency

The underlying logic: HelloChinese builds your foundation. Pleco removes friction from everything else. Du Chinese builds reading fluency through real exposure. And Pimsleur (or a conversation partner) forces your mouth to produce what your brain has learned.

Which App Should You Start With?

Here's the scenario-based answer the rest of the internet dances around.

You're a complete beginner and don't know where to start — Download HelloChinese. Start with the free version. Give it two weeks of consistent daily use (even 15 minutes) before making any decisions about paying.

You travel a lot or commute, and speaking is your primary goal — Pimsleur. Do the 30-minute lessons on your commute. Pair it with Pleco for the vocabulary gap.

You've got some foundation (maybe HSK 2–3) and want to improve your reading — Du Chinese. Start at a level that feels slightly comfortable, not challenging — you want exposure, not frustration.

You're preparing for an HSK exam — HelloChinese Premium+ covers up to HSK 6 vocabulary, and dedicated apps like HSK Lord offer structured exam prep. Supplement with Du Chinese for reading comprehension at your target level.

Your budget is zero — HelloChinese's free tier is genuinely useful up to an intermediate level. Supplement with the free version of Pleco (always) and Duolingo's free tier if you need gamified habit support.

You're choosing an app for your child (ages 8–14) — HelloChinese handles well for motivated older kids. The gamified format is accessible without being childish.

You want one comprehensive course and don't want to manage an "app stack" — Rocket Chinese. It's the most complete self-contained program, even if it's less flashy than the mobile-first apps.

The Bottom Line

If I had to pick just one app for someone starting Mandarin from scratch: HelloChinese. It's designed for this language, it handles the parts that actually trip people up (tones, characters, grammar), and the free tier gives you real value before you commit to anything.

Once you've built that foundation — and you will outgrow HelloChinese eventually, probably around the 6–12 month mark — the app stack approach pays off. Add Du Chinese for reading. Keep Pleco open in the background always. Add Pimsleur or a conversation partner when you're ready to actually talk.

Mandarin isn't a language you'll crack with any single app. But the right tools, used consistently, will get you further than you think.
