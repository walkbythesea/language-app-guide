Best App to Learn Vietnamese in 2026: Honest Picks by Goal

By Elena | April 27, 2026

Vietnamese is one of those languages where a cheerful app can make you feel busy without actually fixing the hard parts. The tones, vowel sounds, and Northern vs Southern pronunciation differences matter early, so the best app to learn Vietnamese is not always the one with the most points, badges, or daily reminders.

When I test language learning apps, I look at them from two sides: as a learner who wants something I can actually keep using, and as a product manager who notices how design choices shape behavior. For Vietnamese, that means I care less about "fun" by itself and more about whether an app helps you hear, repeat, remember, and eventually speak with real feedback.

Quick Answer: The Best Vietnamese Learning Apps

If you want one simple recommendation, Ling is the best app to learn Vietnamese for most complete beginners because it gives you structured, beginner-friendly lessons with native audio, review activities, and enough variety to build a habit.

But Vietnamese is not a one-app language. If your main fear is pronunciation, start with Pimsleur. If you need a real person to correct your tones or dialect, use italki or Preply.

How I Chose These Apps

Most "best app" lists treat Vietnamese like any other language. I do not think that works. For Vietnamese, a pretty interface and a long vocabulary list are not enough.

The most important criteria are:

  • Native speaker audio: You need accurate sound exposure from the start.
  • Tone support: Vietnamese tones change meaning, so vague pronunciation practice is risky.
  • Dialect clarity: Northern and Southern Vietnamese can sound very different.
  • Sentence practice: Isolated vocabulary does not prepare you for real conversations.
  • Review design: Good apps make you recall language, not just recognize it.
  • Human feedback: No app fully replaces a native speaker correcting your pronunciation.

As a product manager, I also pay attention to what an app rewards. If it rewards speed, tapping, and streak protection more than listening carefully, I treat it as a habit tool rather than a complete learning system.

Ling: Best Overall App to Learn Vietnamese for Beginners

Ling is my top pick for most beginners because it gives Vietnamese a more complete app experience than many mainstream platforms. It uses native speaker audio, short games, flashcards, dialogues, sentence exercises, and review activities.

What I like about Ling is that it does not feel like a random vocabulary bucket. The lessons are short, but they still give you a path. If you are starting from zero and do not know what to study first, that structure matters.

Ling also supports many less commonly taught languages, which is one reason it often feels more useful for Vietnamese than apps built mainly around Spanish, French, or German.

What I like:

  • Beginner-friendly lesson path.
  • Native speaker audio for Vietnamese.
  • Dialogues and sentence practice, not only word matching.
  • Gamified design that helps with consistency.
  • A broader course feel than simple flashcard apps.

Where it falls short:

  • Grammar explanations are still lighter than a full course or textbook.
  • Voice games are helpful, but not the same as real pronunciation correction.
  • Advanced learners will likely outgrow it.
  • The playful design may feel too light if you prefer serious lessons.

Pricing: Ling's official pricing page lists plans including $16.99/month, $42.99/3 months, $64.99/6 months, $89.99/year, and $219.99 lifetime, with trial options on some plans. Prices may change or vary by platform.

Bottom line: Choose Ling if you want one organized, beginner-friendly app to start Vietnamese without feeling lost.

Pimsleur: Best for Speaking and Listening

Pimsleur is the app I would choose first if your main goal is to speak out loud. It is audio-led, so you spend less time looking at a screen and more time listening, recalling, and responding.

That matters for Vietnamese. You can recognize words visually and still struggle badly when someone says them at normal speed. Pimsleur's 30-minute lessons force you to build the habit of producing language, not just selecting the right answer.

The product design is not flashy, but that is part of the point. It slows you down enough to listen. For a tonal language, that is often more valuable than racing through tiny exercises.

What I like:

  • Strong focus on speaking from the first lesson.
  • Good for listening, pronunciation awareness, and travel phrases.
  • Works well during walks, commutes, or chores.
  • Includes tools such as flashcards, reading lessons, quizzes, and voice feedback.
  • A 7-day free trial is available.

Where it falls short:

  • It is not the best tool for reading and writing.
  • The lesson style can feel repetitive.
  • Vocabulary coverage is narrower than a large content library.
  • It costs more than many lightweight apps.

Pricing: Pimsleur commonly offers Premium and All Access subscriptions around $19.95-$20.95/month, with annual options and a 7-day trial. Check the official site for current Vietnamese availability and exact pricing.

Bottom line: Choose Pimsleur if you want to sound less hesitant and build speaking confidence early.

Mango Languages: Best for Practical Phrases and Library Access

Mango Languages is a strong option if you like clear, practical lessons. It teaches Vietnamese through conversations, native speaker dialogue, grammar notes, cultural notes, and pronunciation tools.

Mango is less addictive than Ling or Duolingo, but that can be a strength. It feels more like a guided phrase course than a game. If you want to understand how useful expressions fit together, Mango is easier to trust than many tap-and-guess apps.

The biggest reason to check Mango is library access. Many public libraries and schools provide Mango for free, which can make it one of the best-value Vietnamese resources.

What I like:

  • Conversation-based lessons.
  • Native speaker dialogue and pronunciation comparison.
  • Grammar and cultural notes are clearer than many gamified apps.
  • Useful for travel, polite phrases, and daily situations.
  • Library access can make it free.

Where it falls short:

  • It can feel less exciting than newer mobile-first apps.
  • It is not as deep as VietnamesePod101.
  • Speaking feedback is limited compared with a tutor.
  • You may still need another tool for daily review.

Pricing: Mango's official pricing lists an individual plan at $11.99/month or $119.99/year, with family plans and free access through some institutions.

Bottom line: Choose Mango if you want practical conversation lessons and especially if you can access it through a library.

VietnamesePod101: Best for Audio and Lesson Depth

VietnamesePod101 is less like a modern game app and more like a large audio and video lesson library. You get dialogues, lesson notes, transcripts, vocabulary lists, and study tools across different levels.

This is useful because Vietnamese can feel confusing when an app only asks you to infer patterns. Sometimes you need someone to explain what is happening in a sentence. VietnamesePod101 gives you more of that context.

The trade-off is that you need discipline. A huge library can help you, but it can also become a place where you browse more than you study.

What I like:

  • Large Vietnamese audio and video library.
  • Lesson notes and transcripts help with understanding.
  • Free account available.
  • Paid plans add flashcards, line-by-line audio, assessments, and teacher access at higher tiers.
  • Better for self-directed learners than many small apps.

Where it falls short:

  • The amount of content can feel overwhelming.
  • The app experience is not as simple as Ling or Drops.
  • You need to choose a pathway and stick to it.
  • Pronunciation feedback is limited unless you use Premium PLUS or a separate tutor.

Pricing: VietnamesePod101 lists a free plan and paid plans based on longer subscriptions: Basic from $4/month, Premium from $10/month before discounts, and Premium PLUS from $23/month before discounts. The site often runs promotions, so verify the current offer.

Bottom line: Choose VietnamesePod101 if you want depth, explanations, and lots of listening material.

Drops: Best Vietnamese Vocabulary Add-On

Drops is not a complete Vietnamese course. It is a visual vocabulary app, and it does that job well.

The sessions are short, colorful, and easy to finish. This makes Drops useful when you want a low-friction daily habit. It also appears to offer Southern Vietnamese audio in at least some learner-facing materials, which is worth noticing because dialect can matter a lot for Vietnamese.

But from a learning design point of view, Drops is a supplement. It helps you recognize and remember words. It does not give you enough sentence-building, grammar, speaking pressure, or correction to carry your whole study plan.

What I like:

  • Very easy to use for five minutes a day.
  • Strong visual memory support.
  • Native speaker audio.
  • Helpful for building a beginner vocabulary base.
  • Free daily practice is available.

Where it falls short:

  • Mostly vocabulary-focused.
  • Limited grammar and conversation practice.
  • Not enough for pronunciation correction.
  • It can create the feeling of progress without real speaking ability.

Pricing: Drops Premium is listed at $11/month, $69.99/year, or $150 lifetime on its official premium page, though promotions and app-store pricing may vary.

Bottom line: Use Drops as a vocabulary add-on, not your main Vietnamese course.

Duolingo: Best Free Habit Builder, But Not Enough Alone

Duolingo is the easiest free way to start. It is familiar, bite-sized, and good at getting you to come back tomorrow.

That last point matters. As a product manager, I think Duolingo is very good at behavior design. Streaks, reminders, points, and levels reduce the friction of daily practice. The problem is that behavior design is not the same as language depth.

For Vietnamese, user sentiment is often mixed. Learners commonly say Duolingo can help with basic exposure, but it does not explain enough, does not solve pronunciation, and may feel awkward or limited as a main course.

What I like:

  • Free and easy to start.
  • Good for building a daily study habit.
  • Short lessons lower the barrier to practice.
  • Useful for basic vocabulary exposure.

Where it falls short:

  • Tone and pronunciation support are not strong enough.
  • Grammar explanations are limited.
  • It can reward tapping more than careful listening.
  • You will need stronger audio practice and real feedback.

Pricing: Duolingo's free plan costs $0. In the US, Super Duolingo is currently listed around $13.99/month or $95.99/year, while Duolingo Max is around $29.99/month or $167.99/year. Super Family starts around $119.99/year for up to 6 people, Max Family is around $239.99/year, and verified students may see Super annual pricing around $47.99/year. Duolingo prices vary by country and promotion, so read my Duolingo cost guide and full Duolingo review before subscribing.

Bottom line: Duolingo is fine as a free warm-up, but I would not use it as my only Vietnamese resource.

italki and Preply: Best for Real Feedback

At some point, Vietnamese needs a person. italki and Preply connect you with Vietnamese tutors for 1-on-1 lessons.

This is especially important if you care about being understood. A tutor can hear whether your tones are clear, whether your pronunciation matches your target dialect, and whether your sentence sounds natural.

Preply currently lists hundreds of Vietnamese tutors, with filters for price, schedule, and tutor background. italki also lets you browse Vietnamese teachers and choose based on teaching style, accent, availability, and reviews.

What I like:

  • Real correction from native speakers.
  • You can choose Northern, Southern, or other dialect support.
  • Lessons can match your reason for learning: travel, family, work, heritage, or daily life.
  • A weekly lesson creates accountability.

Where it falls short:

  • More expensive than self-study apps if used often.
  • Tutor quality varies.
  • Beginners may feel shy at first.
  • You still need solo practice between lessons.

Pricing: Tutor rates vary widely by platform, teacher, lesson length, and demand. Preply's Vietnamese tutor page shows filters from low-cost lessons to higher-priced tutors, and it lists average Vietnamese tutor rates around the mid-teens per hour, but exact prices change by tutor.

Bottom line: Once you know basic greetings and sentence patterns, a tutor can improve your speaking faster than adding another app subscription.

Which Vietnamese App Should You Choose?

If You Are a Complete Beginner

Start with Ling if you want one app that gives you structure, native audio, review, and enough variety to keep going.

If you want a cheaper start, use Duolingo for a free habit and Drops for vocabulary, but add better audio practice quickly.

If You Mainly Want to Speak Vietnamese

Choose Pimsleur first. It trains the skill that many apps avoid: answering out loud before you feel ready.

After the first few weeks, add a tutor on italki or Preply. For Vietnamese, this is not a luxury if your goal is real conversation. It is how you catch tone and pronunciation mistakes before they become habits.

If You Need Southern Vietnamese

Be careful before subscribing to any app. Many Vietnamese courses lean Northern or do not make dialect differences obvious.

If your reason for learning Vietnamese is connected to Ho Chi Minh City, southern family, a partner, or travel in southern Vietnam, listen to audio samples before paying. A tutor is the safest choice because you can choose someone who teaches the dialect you actually need.

If You Are Learning for Travel

Use Pimsleur for speaking confidence and Mango Languages for practical phrase-based lessons. This combination is more useful than memorizing hundreds of words you cannot pronounce under pressure.

For travel, your goal is not perfect grammar. Your goal is to order food, ask simple questions, understand polite responses, and not freeze when someone answers you.

If You Are on a Tight Budget

Start with free options: Duolingo, Drops' free daily practice, VietnamesePod101's free account, free trials, and Mango if your library offers access.

If you can pay for only one thing, choose based on your weakness: Ling for structure, Pimsleur for speaking, or one tutor lesson if pronunciation is already confusing you.

If You Already Know Basic Vietnamese

Move away from beginner-only apps. Use VietnamesePod101 for listening and explanations, find native content, and book occasional tutor sessions for correction.

At this stage, your problem is probably not "I need another app." It is more likely "I need more real Vietnamese and better feedback."

My Suggested Vietnamese App Stack

If I were building a Vietnamese study routine from scratch, I would not try to find one perfect app. I would build a small stack:

Goal Recommended Stack
First 30 days Ling + Drops
Speaking confidence Pimsleur + weekly tutor
Travel preparation Pimsleur + Mango Languages
Southern Vietnamese Southern tutor + Drops or Mango
Long-term progress VietnamesePod101 + tutor + native content

This is where product design and real learning sometimes pull in different directions. The app that gets you to open it every day is useful, but the app that makes you uncomfortable enough to speak, listen carefully, and get corrected is usually the one that changes your ability.

Frequently Asked Questions

For most beginners, Ling is the best starting app because it gives you structured lessons, native audio, vocabulary review, and beginner-friendly practice. If speaking is your main priority, Pimsleur may be the better first app.

You can build a beginner foundation with apps, especially vocabulary, listening, and basic sentence patterns. But if your goal is real conversation, you will eventually need native speaker feedback through a tutor, language exchange, or immersion.

Duolingo is useful as a free habit builder, but I would not rely on it as your only Vietnamese course. Vietnamese tones, pronunciation, and dialect differences need more focused audio practice and real feedback.

Duolingo is the easiest free starting point. Drops gives free daily vocabulary practice, and VietnamesePod101 has a free account with sample lessons. Mango may also be free if your library provides access.

It depends on your real-life goal. If you will live in Hanoi or use formal learning materials, Northern Vietnamese may make sense. If your family, partner, or travel plans are connected to Ho Chi Minh City or southern Vietnam, look for Southern audio or a Southern tutor.

Ling is better as an all-around beginner app. Pimsleur is better for speaking and listening confidence. If your budget allows, they work well together because they train different skills.

Final Verdict

The best app to learn Vietnamese for most beginners is Ling, but the smartest approach is to combine tools. Use Ling for structure, Pimsleur for speaking, Drops for vocabulary, and a tutor when you need real correction.

Vietnamese rewards careful listening more than fast tapping. If an app helps you slow down, hear native audio clearly, repeat out loud, and get feedback, it deserves a place in your routine.
