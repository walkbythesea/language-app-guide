Best App to Learn Thai in 2026: Honest Picks by Goal and Budget

By Elena | April 26, 2026

Thai is one of those languages where the wrong app can make you feel busy without helping you speak. You can memorize hundreds of words and still freeze when tones, polite particles, or Thai script appear in real life.

If I were starting Thai from zero today, I would not look for one perfect app. I would choose one main app for structure, one tool for listening or pronunciation, and one way to get real feedback from a native speaker.

How I Chose These Thai Learning Apps

For Thai, I care less about streaks and more about whether an app helps with the parts that make Thai hard for English speakers:

  • Tones and pronunciation: Thai has five tones, and small sound changes can change meaning.
  • Listening speed: Native Thai speech can feel fast because words often flow together.
  • Thai script: Romanization helps at the beginning, but it becomes a ceiling later.
  • Useful everyday phrases: Polite particles, social context, and natural word choice matter.
  • Feedback: Apps can drill recognition, but real people catch mistakes differently.

This is also why I would not recommend using only a vocabulary game. Vocabulary matters, but Thai needs ear training and production practice early.

Ling: Best Overall App to Learn Thai for Beginners

Ling is my top pick for most complete beginners because it feels built for languages like Thai. The lessons are short and gamified, but they include native speaker audio, dialogues, pronunciation practice, writing exercises, and review activities.

The biggest advantage is that Ling gives you a gentle path into Thai without immediately overwhelming you. You can start with simple phrases and gradually move into more complex sentence patterns. It also lets you practice multiple skills instead of only matching words to pictures.

What I like:

  • Native speaker audio is useful for hearing tones and rhythm.
  • Lessons are short enough to use every day.
  • It includes Thai among its stronger language offerings.
  • The interface is friendly for beginners who might be intimidated by Thai script.

Where it falls short:

  • Grammar explanations are not as deep as a traditional course.
  • Speech recognition can be imperfect, especially for tonal languages.
  • Some learners may find the style a little too game-like.
  • It is better for beginner-to-lower-intermediate study than advanced Thai.

Pricing: Ling lists monthly, six-month, annual, and lifetime plans. Recent official pricing showed around $16.99/month, $64.99 for 6 months, $89.99/year, and a lifetime option around $149.99. Prices and promotions change, so check Ling's official pricing page before subscribing.

Bottom line: Ling is the best first app if you want one organized place to start Thai and build a daily habit.

Pimsleur: Best Thai App for Speaking and Listening

Pimsleur is not flashy, but it is excellent for one specific thing: getting you to speak out loud. The lessons are audio-based and usually ask you to listen, recall, and respond during a guided conversation.

For Thai, that matters. You need to train your ear before you can reliably produce tones. Pimsleur's slow, repetitive structure can feel old-fashioned, but it forces active recall in a way many tapping-based apps do not.

What I like:

  • It makes you produce Thai aloud from the beginning.
  • The 30-minute audio format works well during walks or commutes.
  • It is strong for travel and survival conversation.
  • Repetition is built into the lesson design, not just added as flashcards.

Where it falls short:

  • It does not teach Thai script in depth.
  • Lessons can feel slow if you prefer visual or fast-paced learning.
  • It is more expensive than many app subscriptions.
  • It works best as a speaking tool, not a complete Thai course.

Pricing: Pimsleur Thai has monthly subscription options, commonly around $14.95/month for audio, $19.95/month for Premium, or $20.95/month for All Access, with lifetime course options also available. Check Pimsleur directly because bundles and discounts change.

Bottom line: If your main goal is to speak understandable Thai on a trip or in daily life, Pimsleur is one of the strongest choices.

ThaiPod101: Best for Audio and Lesson Depth

ThaiPod101 is more like a large online lesson library than a modern gamified app. You get audio and video lessons, transcripts, vocabulary lists, lesson notes, and study tools across different levels.

This format can be very helpful if you like structured explanations. Instead of only asking you to guess patterns, ThaiPod101 gives more context around phrases, culture, and usage. For a language like Thai, that context can save a lot of confusion.

What I like:

  • Huge library of Thai audio and video lessons.
  • Good for learners who want explanations, not only drills.
  • Useful cultural notes and real-life dialogue formats.
  • The free account lets you sample the teaching style.

Where it falls short:

  • The amount of content can feel overwhelming.
  • The app experience is not as smooth as newer mobile-first apps.
  • You need self-discipline to choose a pathway and follow it.
  • Speaking feedback is limited unless you pay for higher tiers.

Pricing: ThaiPod101 has a free lifetime account and paid Basic, Premium, and Premium PLUS tiers. Basic plans can start around $4/month on longer subscriptions, while Premium and Premium PLUS cost more and add features like line-by-line audio, advanced tools, and teacher access.

Bottom line: ThaiPod101 is best if you want a deeper lesson library and do not mind a less game-like learning experience.

StudyThai.ai: Best Newer Thai-Specific Option

StudyThai.ai is more narrowly focused on Thai than broad language apps like Mondly or Drops. That is a real advantage, because Thai learners need help with tones, consonants, vowels, and script in a way that generic apps often under-teach.

From the current information available, StudyThai.ai emphasizes AI-powered practice, spaced repetition, pronunciation training, and Thai phonetics. I would treat it as especially interesting if you want a Thai-first learning tool rather than a language app that happens to include Thai.

What I like:

  • Thai-specific design is a major advantage.
  • Focus on tones, script, and pronunciation fits the real learner problem.
  • Spaced repetition helps with long-term retention.
  • It may be a good alternative if Ling feels too light.

Where it falls short:

  • It is newer, so there is less long-term user feedback than for older apps.
  • Some features may depend on AI feedback, which should not replace native speaker correction.
  • Learners who want a polished mainstream app may prefer Ling or Pimsleur.

Pricing: Recent official pricing showed monthly, yearly, and lifetime-style Pro options, with one page listing around $9.99/month and $69.99/year. I also saw regional pricing references in other sources, so check the official pricing page in your location.

Bottom line: StudyThai.ai is worth considering if your priority is Thai pronunciation and script rather than a generic language-learning experience.

Drops: Best for Thai Vocabulary in Short Sessions

Drops is not a full Thai course, but it is very good at one thing: helping you remember vocabulary through quick, visual practice. If you have five minutes in line at a cafe, Drops is easy to open and finish.

The visual design makes it feel lighter than a textbook, and that can help you stay consistent. But because Drops is mostly vocabulary-focused, I would not use it as your only Thai app.

What I like:

  • Fast, visual, and low-friction.
  • Good for building a daily vocabulary habit.
  • Free plan gives you a small amount of practice every day.
  • Works well as a companion to Ling, Pimsleur, or ThaiPod101.

Where it falls short:

  • It does not teach grammar deeply.
  • It will not make you conversational by itself.
  • Vocabulary without listening and speaking practice is not enough for Thai.
  • Some learners may outgrow it quickly.

Pricing: Drops has a free plan with a daily time limit, often around 5 minutes. Premium pricing is commonly around $13/month, $69.99/year, or $159.99 lifetime, though sales are common.

Bottom line: Use Drops as a vocabulary add-on, not your main Thai teacher.

Mango Languages: Best for Travel Phrases and Library Access

Mango Languages is a practical choice if you want usable phrases and explanations without a heavily gamified interface. It teaches through conversation chunks, grammar notes, and pronunciation comparison.

One reason Mango is worth checking is that many public libraries and institutions provide free access. If your library offers it, Mango can be one of the best-value options for Thai.

What I like:

  • Phrase-based lessons are useful for travel.
  • Grammar and culture notes are clearer than many gamified apps.
  • Voice comparison helps you hear how close you are to native audio.
  • Library access can make it free for some learners.

Where it falls short:

  • It may feel less exciting than Ling or Drops.
  • It is not as Thai-specialized as StudyThai.ai or ThaiPod101.
  • Advanced learners will probably need more resources.

Pricing: Mango's consumer pricing is commonly listed around $7.99/month or $79.99/year for one language, and $17.99/month or $179.99/year for all languages. Before paying, check whether your local library gives you access.

Bottom line: Mango is a sensible option for travel phrases, especially if you can use it through a library.

italki and Preply: Best for Real Thai Conversation

Apps can help you prepare, but at some point Thai needs a real person. Platforms like italki and Preply connect you with Thai tutors for 1-on-1 lessons.

This is where you get feedback that apps usually miss: whether your tones are understandable, whether your sentence sounds too formal, and whether you are using the right polite particle for the situation.

What I like:

  • Real-time correction from native speakers.
  • You can focus on your actual goals, such as travel, dating, work, or family.
  • Tutors can explain tone mistakes and pronunciation habits.
  • Lessons give you accountability.

Where it falls short:

  • More expensive than self-study apps if used often.
  • Tutor quality varies, so you may need to try a few.
  • It can feel intimidating for shy beginners.

Pricing: Tutor platforms vary by teacher. italki is usually pay-per-lesson, with many lessons starting around $4-$25 depending on tutor, length, and experience. Preply tutor rates also vary and often use a recurring lesson model. Always check the tutor profile before booking.

Bottom line: Once you can introduce yourself and ask basic questions, a weekly tutor session will do more for your speaking than another app subscription.

Which Thai App Should You Choose?

If You Are a Complete Beginner

Start with Ling if you want a friendly app that covers several skills. Add Drops only if you enjoy vocabulary games and need extra daily momentum.

If you are serious from the beginning, pair Ling with Pimsleur. Ling gives you visual structure; Pimsleur forces you to answer out loud.

If You Mainly Want to Speak Thai for Travel

Choose Pimsleur first, then add Mango Languages or Ling for phrase review. This combination is more practical than memorizing isolated vocabulary lists.

For travel, your goal is not perfect grammar. Your goal is being able to order food, ask directions, use polite language, and understand simple replies.

If You Want to Learn Thai Script

Look at StudyThai.ai and Thai-specific resources before relying only on broad language apps. Thai script deserves focused attention, and romanization will eventually become a crutch.

Some learners also like dedicated Thai script apps, but I would only link or recommend a specific one after checking the current app-store listing and update status.

If You Like Structured Lessons

Choose ThaiPod101. It has more depth than most mobile-first apps and gives you explanations instead of only exercises. The downside is that you need to organize your own path and stay consistent.

If You Are on a Tight Budget

Start with free trials and free tiers: Ling's free access, Drops' daily free practice, ThaiPod101's free account, and any Mango access through your library.

If you can pay for only one thing, I would choose based on your weakness: Ling for an all-around beginner course, Pimsleur for speaking, or a tutor lesson if you already know some basics.

If You Already Know Basic Thai

Move toward italki or Preply. Apps are useful for review, but intermediate progress comes from using Thai with real people, hearing natural corrections, and learning how Thai is actually spoken.

My Suggested Thai App Stack

If you want a simple plan, I would use this:

Goal Recommended Stack
First 30 days Ling + Drops
Speaking confidence Pimsleur + Ling
Script and tones StudyThai.ai + ThaiPod101
Travel preparation Pimsleur + Mango Languages
Long-term progress ThaiPod101 + weekly italki or Preply lesson

Frequently Asked Questions

For most beginners, Ling is the best starting app because it combines short lessons, native speaker audio, pronunciation practice, and beginner-friendly review. If speaking is your main priority, Pimsleur may be the better first app.

You can build a strong beginner foundation with apps, especially vocabulary, listening, and basic phrases. But if your goal is real conversation, you will eventually need native speaker feedback through a tutor, language exchange, or immersion.

Thai is harder than many app-friendly languages because tones, vowel length, and script matter a lot. Apps can help, but choose tools that include audio, pronunciation, and Thai-specific explanations rather than only vocabulary games.

Drops is useful for free daily vocabulary practice, ThaiPod101 has a free account with sample lessons, and Ling offers limited free access. For serious learning, the best free route is usually combining several free tiers with YouTube or library resources like Mango if available.

Romanization is helpful at the beginning, but you should learn Thai script if you want to go beyond travel phrases. Thai spelling, tones, and pronunciation make more sense once you understand the script.

Ling is better as an all-around beginner app. Pimsleur is better for speaking and listening confidence. If your budget allows, they work well together because they train different skills.
