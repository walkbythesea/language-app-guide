Busuu Review 2026: Is It Worth Paying For?

By Elena | May 4, 2026

Busuu sits in an interesting middle ground: more structured than Duolingo, more social than Babbel, and less chaotic than many AI-first language apps. If you are looking for a serious but still approachable language app, the real question is not whether Busuu is "good." It is whether its teaching style, community feedback, and paid plans match the way you actually learn.

Quick Verdict

Busuu is one of the better language apps for learners who want structure, grammar, and real feedback, but its value depends heavily on the price you are offered.

The strongest part of Busuu is its course design. Lessons feel more intentional than the tap-and-translate loops you see in many gamified apps. You get vocabulary, grammar, listening, writing, speaking prompts, and community corrections from other learners or native speakers. That combination makes Busuu feel closer to a guided course than a language game.

The weaknesses are also clear. The free version is limited and ad-supported. Pricing can vary a lot between web, iOS, Android, country, and promotion. Some users also report subscription or syncing issues, especially in app store reviews. Busuu is still a strong pick, but I would test the free version carefully and compare the website price against the mobile app price before subscribing.

What Is Busuu?

Busuu is a language learning app and website that combines expert-designed courses with feedback from a large learner community. Busuu says its community includes more than 120 million learners, and the app is currently available for 14 languages: English, Spanish, French, Japanese, German, Italian, Korean, Arabic, Russian, Turkish, Chinese, Portuguese, Dutch, and Polish.

The app is now part of Chegg, and it positions itself less as a game and more as a practical course for people who want to speak with confidence. Lessons are short, but they usually have a clear teaching purpose: introduce vocabulary, explain a grammar point, train recognition, then ask you to produce language through writing or speaking.

That structure matters. As a product manager, I pay attention to whether an app is optimizing for daily engagement or actual learning progression. Busuu still uses streaks, reminders, and premium prompts, but the core course feels more learning-led than dopamine-led.

How Busuu Works

Busuu starts by asking which language you want to learn and why. From there, it builds a course path with bite-sized lessons, review sessions, and practice activities. You can study casually, but the app works best if you treat it like a lightweight course and complete lessons in order.

The typical lesson flow includes:

  • New words and phrases
  • Short listening or reading activities
  • Grammar notes
  • Multiple-choice and fill-in exercises
  • Writing or speaking prompts
  • Review practice through Smart Review
  • Optional feedback from the Busuu Community

The biggest difference from Duolingo is that Busuu explains more. Duolingo often expects you to infer patterns through repetition. Busuu is more willing to stop and say, "Here is the grammar point." If you are an adult learner who likes knowing why a sentence works, that is a real advantage.

Key Features

Structured Courses

Busuu's courses are organized around levels and practical topics. You are not just collecting random vocabulary; you are moving through units that build toward everyday communication. Depending on the language, the course may include beginner, intermediate, and upper-intermediate material, though depth varies by language.

This is where Busuu feels stronger than many casual apps. The lessons have enough structure to make progress feel coherent, but they are not so academic that you feel like you are reading a textbook on your phone.

For Spanish, French, German, and English learners, Busuu tends to feel especially polished. For some other languages, the course may be useful but less deep. That is common across language apps, but it is worth remembering: "Busuu is good" does not mean every Busuu language course is equally strong.

Community Feedback

Busuu's community correction system is its most distinctive feature. You complete a writing or speaking exercise, submit it, and other users can correct or comment on your answer. In return, you can help people learning your native language.

This changes the feeling of the app. Instead of only interacting with machine-graded exercises, you get occasional human input. That is valuable because language is messy. A sentence can be technically correct but unnatural, or understandable but awkward. A native speaker can notice those details better than a simple quiz engine.

There are limits. Community feedback quality depends on who responds, how active the language pair is, and whether the person correcting you explains their reasoning. You should not treat every correction as perfect. But as a low-friction way to get real feedback, it is one of Busuu's best ideas.

Grammar and Vocabulary Review

Busuu includes review tools for vocabulary and grammar, with Premium unlocking more complete access. The official Premium feature list includes Grammar Review, Vocabulary Review, offline mode, ad-free learning, certificates, and the ability to skip lessons.

The review system is useful because it saves weak items and brings them back later. It is not as customizable as Anki, and serious learners may still want a separate flashcard system. But for most app-based learners, Busuu's review tools are good enough to prevent the common problem of "I completed the lesson and forgot everything two days later."

AI Conversations and Pronunciation Feedback

Busuu's Premium Plus tier adds AI Conversations, pronunciation feedback, Mistake Repair, exclusive courses, and streak protection. The idea is to give learners more speaking practice without the pressure of talking to a real person immediately.

This is the kind of feature that looks small but changes daily use. Many learners avoid speaking because it feels embarrassing. A patient AI partner can help you rehearse before you try a tutor, a language exchange partner, or a real-life conversation.

That said, AI conversation practice should be treated as a bridge, not the destination. It can help you form sentences and build confidence, but it does not replace real human conversation. Busuu's community feedback is still important because it brings human judgment into the loop.

If AI speaking practice is your main priority, Busuu is not the only option to compare. I would also look at Langua, which is built more directly around open-ended AI conversation, Call Mode, and detailed speaking feedback.

Certificates

Busuu offers certificates for completed course levels to Premium and Premium Plus subscribers. These are aligned with the CEFR framework and can be downloaded after you complete the relevant level.

For most learners, these certificates are better for motivation than formal proof. They may be useful for personal tracking or showing progress, but they are not the same as an official exam like DELE, DELF, Goethe-Zertifikat, IELTS, or JLPT. Still, as a progress marker inside the app, they make the learning path feel more concrete.

Busuu Pricing

Busuu has a free plan, Premium, and Premium Plus. The official support page says pricing varies depending on platform, local currency, and active offers, so there is no single universal price I would trust forever.

On the U.S. App Store listing I checked, visible in-app purchase examples included monthly subscriptions around $6.99 to $23.49 and annual Premium or Premium Plus options ranging roughly from $70 to $139.99. Other review sources and users report different annual prices, especially during promotions. Google Play reviews also mention renewal charges around the $60 range, but app store pricing can change at any time.

Busuu Pros and Cons

Pros

  • More structured than many language apps: Busuu feels like a course, not just a daily puzzle.
  • Good grammar support: The explanations are helpful for learners who want to understand patterns instead of guessing.
  • Community feedback is genuinely useful: Getting corrections from real people adds something most apps cannot fully automate.
  • Strong review tools: Smart Review helps you revisit weak vocabulary and grammar.
  • Good for speaking confidence: Community exercises, pronunciation feedback, and AI Conversations all push you toward production.
  • High app store ratings: Busuu has a 4.7 rating on both the App Store and Google Play at the time of research.

Cons

  • Pricing is inconsistent: Costs can vary by platform, region, promotion, and subscription tier.
  • The free version is limited: It is fine for testing, but you will likely hit restrictions if you want sustained progress.
  • Only 14 languages: That is enough for many learners, but far fewer than Duolingo or Memrise.
  • Course depth varies by language: Popular European languages tend to feel stronger than some smaller courses.
  • Subscription complaints exist: Recent app store reviews include complaints about renewal charges, login problems, syncing, and cancellation frustration.
  • Not enough alone for fluency: Busuu is a strong app, but you still need real conversation, listening input, and broader reading or media exposure.

Who Is Busuu Best For?

Beginners who want structure: Busuu is a very good fit. It gives you a path, explains grammar, and asks you to actively produce language instead of only recognizing answers.

Learners who dislike overly gamified apps: If Duolingo feels too much like a game, Busuu may feel calmer and more adult. For a deeper comparison of mainstream apps, see Duolingo vs Babbel and the language app comparison hub.

People who want feedback from real humans: Busuu's community corrections are not perfect, but they are valuable. This is especially helpful for writing and pronunciation confidence.

Spanish learners: Busuu is a strong option for Spanish, especially if you want grammar and speaking practice. You can also compare it with other choices in our guide to the best apps to learn Spanish.

Price-sensitive learners: Be careful. Busuu can be good value on a discounted annual plan, but it is less attractive if you are shown a high monthly price. Check the pricing hub for broader cost comparisons across language apps.

Intermediate learners: Busuu can help, but it may not be enough as your main resource. At that stage, you should add podcasts, graded readers, tutors, conversation exchanges, or native content.

If your biggest problem is consistency, Duolingo may still win. If your biggest problem is understanding grammar and forming your own sentences, Busuu is stronger.

Is Busuu Worth It?

Busuu is worth it if you want a structured app that teaches grammar, gives you review tools, and nudges you toward speaking and writing. It is especially worthwhile if you can get a reasonable annual price and you are studying one of Busuu's stronger languages.

I would not pay a high monthly price for Busuu unless you are testing Premium Plus for a specific reason, such as AI Conversations or pronunciation feedback. For most learners, the sweet spot is the annual Premium plan during a discount, after you have used the free version long enough to know you like the teaching style.

From a learner's point of view, Busuu feels less flashy than many apps, but that is part of its appeal. It is not trying to entertain you every second. It is trying to move you through a course and make you produce language. That is a good trade-off for serious beginners.

Final Rating

Overall: 4 / 5 — Busuu is a well-designed language learning app with strong structure, useful grammar support, and one of the better community feedback systems available. Its biggest weakness is not the learning method; it is the uncertainty around pricing and the limits of the free plan.
