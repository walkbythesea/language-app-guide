Memrise Review 2026: Is It Still Worth It?

By Elena | April 30, 2026

Memrise has been around for over a decade, and it's gone through more identity changes than most apps survive. It started as a community-powered flashcard platform beloved by polyglots. Now it's an AI-driven language app pitching itself at travelers and exam preppers. Whether that transformation is an upgrade or a loss depends entirely on what you're looking for — and when you first discovered it.

Quick Verdict

Memrise is one of the better vocabulary-building apps available — but it's no longer the platform many long-time users fell in love with.

The native speaker video clips are genuinely useful for training your ear on real accents. The spaced repetition system works. And the newer AI conversation features give you a low-pressure way to practice speaking. But the removal of community-created courses — once Memrise's biggest draw — has left a gap that the polished official content doesn't fully fill. The annual Pro plan is more reasonable than the monthly price, but if you need grammar instruction or structured progression, you'll need to look elsewhere.

What Is Memrise?

Memrise is a language learning app founded in 2010 by neuroscientist Ed Cooke and language enthusiast Ben Whately. The original idea was clever: combine spaced repetition (a proven memory technique) with user-created "mems" — mnemonics, images, and memory aids that the community built together. For years, this community-driven approach was what made Memrise special. Users created thousands of courses covering everything from Mandarin HSK vocabulary to ancient Greek.

But the Memrise of 2026 is a fundamentally different product. Here's what's changed:

  • Community courses removed from the main app: In 2024, Memrise moved all user-created courses to a separate website (community-courses.memrise.com). They're no longer accessible in the mobile app. This was, and remains, the most controversial change in Memrise's history.
  • AI features front and center: The "MemBot" — an AI chatbot powered by GPT — lets you practice conversations. "AI Buddies" offer scenario-based role-play. These are now core to the Memrise experience.
  • Native speaker videos: Memrise has invested heavily in short video clips of real people speaking naturally — slang, speed, emotion included. This is arguably its strongest differentiator.
  • Exam prep: A newer addition targeting students preparing for IELTS, GCSE, A-Level, and CEFR-aligned exams.
  • New CEO: Ben became CEO and has been engaging directly with the community on Reddit, promising to explore bringing back some course creation features.

Memrise currently offers official courses in approximately 35 languages, including Spanish (both Mexico and Spain varieties), French, German, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Arabic, Hindi, Turkish, Vietnamese, Thai, and several less commonly taught languages like Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, and Icelandic.

My Experience Testing Memrise

When I first opened Memrise, the native speaker videos immediately stood out. Instead of a robotic text-to-speech voice, you watch a real person — on a street, in a café, at home — saying the phrase naturally. As someone who pays attention to how apps teach pronunciation, this felt like a genuine advantage. You're not just learning words; you're learning how those words actually sound in someone's mouth.

The spaced repetition engine does its job well. Words I got wrong came back more frequently, and words I consistently nailed gradually spaced out. After a couple of weeks of daily use, I could feel vocabulary sticking in a way that felt durable rather than cramming-for-a-test temporary.

Where my enthusiasm cooled was in the overall learning structure — or rather, the lack of it. Memrise teaches you words and phrases, but it doesn't really teach you language. There's almost no grammar instruction. You'll learn how to say "I want a coffee" but not understand why the words are in that order, or how to modify the sentence for "she wanted two coffees." For a product person, that's a structural gap: the app builds recognition but doesn't build generation — the ability to create your own sentences from scratch.

The interface also takes some getting used to. Features feel scattered across different sections, and I've seen reports that certain features (like specific video exercises or grammar drills) appear on one device but not another. That kind of inconsistency erodes trust.

The AI conversation features are a mixed bag. The MemBot works for simple exchanges and gives you a low-stakes way to practice forming sentences. But the conversations can feel shallow — it's more like a chatbot exercise than a real interaction. If you've used ChatGPT or similar tools for language practice, you'll find Memrise's version fairly basic.

Key Features Breakdown

Native Speaker Videos

This is Memrise's standout feature, and it's genuinely good. You watch short clips of real native speakers saying words and phrases in natural contexts — different accents, different speaking speeds, informal registers. It trains your ear in a way that most language apps simply don't.

For languages where pronunciation is a major barrier — like Mandarin tones, Vietnamese tones, or Japanese pitch accent — hearing multiple real speakers is invaluable. Textbook audio and TTS voices just can't replicate this.

Spaced Repetition System

Memrise's SRS is solid and works largely as you'd expect. New items are introduced, reviewed at increasing intervals, and items you struggle with are surfaced more frequently. It's the same proven approach that makes Anki effective, wrapped in a more approachable interface.

The difference from Anki is that you don't build your own cards (at least not in the main app anymore). You're working through Memrise's curated content, which means less customization but also less setup friction.

MemBot and AI Buddies

The MemBot lets you have text-based or voice-based conversations with an AI in your target language. AI Buddies extend this with scenario-based role-plays — ordering at a restaurant, checking into a hotel, making small talk at a party.

These features are useful for learners who feel anxious about speaking with real people. The AI is patient, doesn't judge, and is available anytime. But the conversations lack the unpredictability and depth of real human interaction. They're a stepping stone, not a replacement for actual conversation practice.

If what you really want is deeper AI speaking practice, I would compare Memrise with Langua. Langua is more focused on open-ended conversation, Call Mode, and correction after you speak, while Memrise is still strongest as a vocabulary and listening app.

Gamification

Memrise uses points, streaks, and progress tracking to keep you coming back. It's less aggressive than Duolingo's gamification — you won't get guilt-trip notifications from a cartoon owl — but it's also less effective at building ironclad habits. The motivation mechanics feel lighter, which is either a plus or a minus depending on your personality.

What's Missing: Grammar

This is the elephant in the room. Memrise has added some verb conjugation drills and sentence-building exercises, but there's no systematic grammar instruction. You won't find explanations of tense formation, word order rules, or case systems. If you're learning a grammatically complex language like German or Russian, this is a significant limitation.

Some users are fine absorbing grammar patterns through exposure. But many adult learners — especially those coming from languages with very different structures — need at least some explicit explanation. Memrise doesn't provide it.

The Community Course Controversy

I need to address this directly because it's the single biggest source of user frustration with Memrise in recent years.

For most of its history, Memrise's greatest asset was its community. Users created thousands of courses — some covering niche topics no commercial app would ever touch, some meticulously maintained with custom audio and mnemonics. Many users chose Memrise specifically for these community courses.

In 2024, Memrise removed community courses from the main app and moved them to a separate website. The rationale was presumably to focus the app on professionally produced content and AI features. But for long-time users, it felt like the thing that made Memrise Memrise was being taken away.

The reaction on Reddit and Trustpilot has been harsh. Memrise's Trustpilot rating sits at roughly 1.7 out of 5, with a significant portion of negative reviews citing this change. On Reddit, users frequently recommend alternatives like Anki or the community course website for serious vocabulary study.

The new CEO has acknowledged this frustration and mentioned exploring ways to bring back some course creation features. But as of early 2026, community courses remain separate from the main app.

From a product perspective, I understand the business logic — maintaining user-generated content is expensive and hard to quality-control. But the execution alienated Memrise's most loyal users, and that's a costly mistake for any product.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Best-in-class native speaker videos: Real people, real accents, real contexts — no other major language app does this as well
  • Effective spaced repetition: The core memory system works and vocabulary sticks
  • 35 official languages: Including several less commonly taught ones like Icelandic, Yoruba, and Mongolian
  • AI conversation practice: Low-pressure speaking practice that's available anytime
  • Good for beginners: The onboarding is smooth and you start learning useful phrases quickly
  • Exam prep modules: A practical addition for students preparing for IELTS, GCSE, or CEFR exams

Cons

  • No real grammar instruction: You'll learn phrases but won't understand the underlying rules
  • Community courses removed: The feature that once defined Memrise is no longer in the main app
  • Expensive monthly plan: The tested monthly Pro price is $24.99, which is steep for a vocabulary-focused app
  • Interface can be cluttered: Features feel scattered, and some experiences vary between devices
  • AI conversations are shallow: The MemBot works for basics but lacks conversational depth
  • Not enough for intermediate or advanced learners: The content thins out quickly once you're past beginner level

Who Is Memrise Best For?

Beginners building initial vocabulary: Great fit. Memrise excels at helping you learn and retain your first few hundred words and phrases in a new language. The native speaker videos make this process feel more connected to real-world usage than most competitors.

Travelers preparing for a trip: Solid choice. If you need practical phrases and want to understand how locals actually sound, Memrise's video-based approach is ideal. You'll learn to recognize real speech patterns, not just textbook pronunciation.

Visual and audio learners: Good match. If you learn better from seeing and hearing real people than from reading text on a screen, Memrise's format plays to your strengths.

Students preparing for language exams: Worth trying. The newer exam prep features for IELTS, GCSE, and CEFR-aligned tests add practical value if you have a specific test coming up.

Intermediate learners wanting to push further: Probably not enough. Once you've built basic vocabulary, Memrise doesn't offer the grammar depth or structured progression to take you to the next level. You'll want to supplement with other resources.

Users who loved the old community courses: You'll likely be disappointed. If community-created content was your reason for using Memrise, the current app is a different product. Check the separate community courses website, or consider alternatives that offer more customization.

Memrise Pricing

Based on my latest in-app test, Memrise Pro was priced at $24.99/month, $61.99/year, or $164.99 for lifetime access. The lifetime option was shown as a 50% discount from $329.99.

These prices are from my latest in-app test. Memrise pricing may still vary by platform, region, promotion, and currency. The app also notes that Pro features are available in most supported languages.

My take on the pricing: The annual plan is the only option that makes clear financial sense for most learners. At $61.99/year, it comes out to about $5.17/month, which is much easier to justify if you use Memrise regularly. The $24.99 monthly plan is steep for what you get, especially compared with competitors. The $164.99 lifetime plan is tempting if you already know you like Memrise, but it's still a gamble: Memrise has changed direction dramatically before, and there's no guarantee the features you're paying for today will still exist in two years.

The free plan is usable but limited. You'll hit walls on video access and AI conversations fairly quickly. It's enough to test whether the Memrise approach works for you, but not enough for sustained learning.

If you're price-sensitive, it's worth noting that Duolingo's free tier offers more content without a paywall (though it comes with energy limits and ads). And Anki — the gold standard for spaced repetition — is completely free on desktop and Android.

How Memrise Compares

Memrise vs Duolingo: Duolingo is more gamified, has better habit-building mechanics, and offers a more generous free tier. Memrise has better pronunciation training through native speaker videos and a less aggressive monetization feel (no energy system). Duolingo is stronger on grammar (barely), while Memrise is stronger on teaching you how language actually sounds. If you're a complete beginner who needs motivation, Duolingo. If you want to train your ear and build vocabulary with real-world audio, Memrise.

Memrise vs Busuu: Busuu is stronger if you want a structured course, grammar review, and human feedback on writing or speaking exercises. Memrise is better for vocabulary, listening exposure, and native speaker video clips. If you need an app to guide your overall study path, Busuu is the more complete option.

Memrise vs Anki: Anki is free (desktop and Android), infinitely customizable, and the serious language learner's tool of choice for spaced repetition. But it has a steep learning curve and zero hand-holding. Memrise is Anki with training wheels — less powerful, more polished, and much easier to start using immediately. If you're willing to invest time in setup, Anki wins on flexibility. If you want something that works out of the box, Memrise is more approachable.

Memrise vs Babbel: Babbel offers structured, grammar-focused lessons that feel more like a traditional language course. It's better for learners who want to understand why a language works the way it does, not just memorize phrases. Memrise is better for vocabulary breadth and listening comprehension.
