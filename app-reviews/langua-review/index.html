Langua Review 2026: Is This AI Language Tutor Worth It?

By Elena | May 4, 2026

Langua is not trying to be another cute daily quiz app. It is trying to solve one of the biggest problems in language learning: getting enough speaking practice when you do not have a tutor, exchange partner, or confidence yet. When I test apps like this, I pay close attention to whether the AI actually helps you produce language, or whether it just creates the feeling of progress.

Quick Verdict

Langua is one of the strongest AI language apps for speaking practice, especially if you are stuck between beginner lessons and real conversation.

Its biggest advantage is simple: it gives you a realistic, low-pressure place to speak. You can have voice conversations with AI characters, use Call Mode hands-free, get corrections, save vocabulary, and review your mistakes afterward. That makes Langua much more useful for speaking confidence than traditional apps built around tapping, matching, and multiple choice.

But Langua is not cheap, and it is not a complete language course in the traditional sense. If you are a total beginner who needs step-by-step grammar, or if you mainly want free lessons, you may be better off starting with something like Duolingo or Busuu. Langua makes the most sense once you already know some words and want to start using them out loud.

What Is Langua?

Langua is an AI language learning app from LanguaTalk, a company originally known for connecting learners with human language tutors. Langua is its AI product: a speaking-focused coach available on web, iOS, and Android.

The app is built around conversation. Instead of completing short translation drills, you talk with AI characters in your target language. You can choose topics, role-plays, debates, grammar practice, vocabulary-focused chats, or more open-ended conversations. Langua also includes Call Mode, which lets you have a hands-free voice conversation while walking, driving, or doing chores.

Officially, Langua supports 20+ languages, including Spanish, English, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Swedish, Romanian, Russian, Greek, Turkish, Hindi, Danish, Norwegian, Finnish, Polish, Czech, Croatian, Japanese, Korean, and Mandarin Chinese. Your subscription includes access to multiple languages, so you are not locked into one course.

That makes Langua different from many older language apps. It is less about completing a fixed curriculum and more about creating practice opportunities. If Memrise is strongest for vocabulary and native speaker clips, Langua is strongest for getting you to speak more often.

How Langua Works

Langua starts by letting you choose your target language and practice style. You can type, speak, listen, save vocabulary, and get corrections during or after the conversation. The app adapts to your level, so beginners can use suggestions and translations, while intermediate learners can hold more natural conversations.

The main learning loop looks like this:

Step What Happens Why It Matters
Choose a conversation Pick a topic, role-play, grammar drill, or free chat You practice language in a situation, not just as isolated words
Speak or type Respond naturally in your target language You train production, which many apps neglect
Get help when stuck Use translations, reply suggestions, or your native language This lowers the fear of freezing mid-conversation
Review corrections See mistakes, explanations, transcripts, and reports You turn conversation into feedback, not just chat
Save vocabulary Add useful words to flashcards and stories New words come from your own conversations

As a product manager, I like this loop because it connects practice, feedback, and review. Many AI chat tools are fun for a few days but do not create a learning system. Langua feels more intentional: you talk, you get corrected, you save words, and the app can bring those words back later.

Key Features

AI Conversation Practice

Langua's core feature is open conversation with AI tutors that sound more natural than the robotic voices many learners are used to. The official site says its voices are cloned from native speakers, and the app offers multiple dialect options depending on the language.

This matters because speaking practice is partly emotional. If an app sounds awkward, slow, or obviously synthetic, you do not fully relax into the conversation. Langua's voices are not the same as a real person, but they are good enough to make practice feel more alive.

You can use Langua for casual chats, travel situations, work topics, debates, grammar practice, and custom scenarios. That flexibility is useful if you are tired of repeating beginner sentences like "the apple is red" and want to practice language you might actually use.

Call Mode

Call Mode is one of Langua's strongest ideas. Instead of sitting in front of a lesson screen, you can have a hands-free voice conversation. For many learners, this changes when practice can happen. A 10-minute walk can become speaking practice. A commute can become a review session.

This is the kind of feature that looks small but changes daily use. The easier it is to start speaking, the more likely you are to do it consistently.

There is one caveat: voice technology is never frictionless for everyone. Google Play reviews include both very positive comments about practicing in the car and complaints about microphone issues or having to repeat answers. I would test Call Mode carefully during the free access period before paying.

Corrections and Feedback

Langua offers several kinds of feedback: written corrections, explanations, verbal coaching, post-chat reports, transcripts, and audio summaries. You can also review words and phrases you struggled with after a conversation.

This is where Langua feels more educational than a general chatbot. A normal AI chat can keep the conversation going, but it may not stop to help you understand why your sentence sounded unnatural. Langua is designed to catch mistakes and turn them into learning moments.

The feedback will not always replace a human teacher. A real tutor can notice patterns across your learning, adjust explanations, and understand your goals in a deeper way. But for daily practice, Langua's correction system is useful because it is immediate and low-pressure.

Vocabulary Tools

Langua does not treat vocabulary as a separate flashcard island. You can save words from your conversations, see contextual translations, review them with spaced repetition flashcards, and practice them in AI-generated stories or vocabulary-focused chats.

That is a smart design choice. Vocabulary sticks better when it comes from something you tried to say, not from a random list. If you keep forgetting a phrase during conversation, saving and reusing it makes more sense than memorizing words you may never need.

Beginner Support

Langua is not only for intermediate learners. It includes guided support for lower levels, including reply suggestions, slower audio, translations, and the ability to switch to your native language when stuck. The App Store update notes also mention slow audio speed controls and message editing, which are helpful for learners who need more time.

Still, I would not call Langua the easiest first app for a complete beginner. If you know almost nothing in your target language, a structured app such as Busuu may feel clearer at the start because it teaches vocabulary, grammar, and lesson progression more directly. Langua becomes more powerful once you have enough basic language to participate.

Langua Pricing

Langua has a limited free version and two paid Pro plans: Standard and Unlimited. Pricing can change by platform, country, currency, and promotion, so check the official site before subscribing.

At the time of my research, the U.S. App Store showed these in-app purchase examples:

Plan Visible App Store Price Main Limit
Langua Pro Communicate Monthly $19.99/month Standard-style access
Langua Pro Communicate Annual $149.99/year Standard-style access
Langua Pro Unlimited Monthly $29.99/month Unlimited-style access
Langua Pro Unlimited Annual $199.99/year Unlimited-style access
3-month options $49.99-$69.99 Depends on tier

Langua's official support page says the Standard plan includes up to 75 regular chat messages per day or 30 minutes of Call Mode daily. The free version has low limits: about 20 messages per week in standard chat modes and 3 minutes per day of Call Mode. Unlimited removes those practice limits.

Langua also offers a free trial for Pro access, but the official support page says a card is required for the full trial. The trial is 5 days for a monthly plan or 7 days for an annual plan. Langua also mentions a 30-day money-back guarantee for web or Android subscriptions, but not App Store subscriptions because Apple controls refunds.

My pricing advice: if you are serious about speaking practice, the annual Standard plan is probably the most reasonable starting point. I would only choose Unlimited if you already know you will use Call Mode heavily or practice for long sessions. If you are just curious, test the free conversation and trial before paying.

Pros and Cons

Pros

  • Excellent for speaking confidence: Langua gives you a private place to speak out loud without embarrassment.
  • More flexible than scripted apps: You can practice role-plays, debates, grammar, vocabulary, or open conversation.
  • Call Mode is genuinely useful: Hands-free practice makes it easier to fit speaking into daily life.
  • Feedback is built into the flow: Corrections, reports, transcripts, and summaries help you learn from conversations.
  • Vocabulary tools are contextual: Saved words come from your own chats and can reappear in practice.
  • Good for intermediate plateau learners: If you understand lessons but freeze in conversation, Langua directly targets that gap.

Cons

  • The free version is very limited: It is enough to test the app, not enough for serious practice.
  • Paid plans are not cheap: Langua costs more than many traditional language apps.
  • Not a full structured course: You may still need grammar explanations, reading practice, or a separate study plan.
  • AI is still AI: Conversations can occasionally feel less interactive than a real tutor, and it may not catch every nuance.
  • Voice experience may vary: Some users report microphone or recording frustration, especially on mobile.
  • Better after the basics: Absolute beginners may need a more guided course first.

Who Should Use Langua?

Langua is best for learners who already know some basics and want to speak more. If you can form simple sentences but avoid conversation because you feel slow, embarrassed, or unprepared, Langua is a strong fit.

It is also a good choice for intermediate learners who feel stuck. This is the classic language app problem: you can complete lessons, recognize vocabulary, and understand simple audio, but real conversation still feels far away. Langua gives you repetitions that traditional apps rarely provide.

You should consider Langua if:

  • You want daily speaking practice without booking a tutor.
  • You feel anxious speaking with real people.
  • You learn better through conversation than drills.
  • You want corrections and explanations, not just chat.
  • You are learning a language supported by Langua and care about pronunciation or listening confidence.

You may want to skip Langua, or use it later, if:

  • You need a free app for long-term study.
  • You are an absolute beginner who wants a step-by-step course.
  • You prefer human correction from tutors or native speakers.
  • You mainly want grammar lessons, reading, or exam prep.
  • You get frustrated when voice recognition is imperfect.

For Spanish learners specifically, Langua can pair well with a broader app stack. You might use a structured course from our best apps to learn Spanish guide, then add Langua for speaking practice. Langua vs Other Language Apps

Langua is easiest to understand when compared with more familiar apps:

App Best For Weak Spot
Langua AI speaking practice and feedback Less structured as a full course
Duolingo Free habit-building and beginner vocabulary Limited real conversation practice
Busuu Structured lessons, grammar, and community feedback Smaller language list and variable pricing
Memrise Vocabulary and native speaker videos Less grammar depth and lighter conversation practice
Human tutor Personalized correction and accountability Higher cost and scheduling friction

I would not treat Langua as a Duolingo replacement for every learner. It solves a different problem. Duolingo helps you show up daily. Busuu gives you more course structure. Memrise helps vocabulary stick with real speaker clips. Langua helps you speak more often.

For many learners, the best setup is not one app. It is one app for structure, one source for listening or reading, and one channel for speaking. Langua can be the speaking channel.

Is Langua Worth It?

Langua is worth it if speaking practice is your bottleneck. If you already have vocabulary and grammar knowledge but rarely speak, the app offers something more valuable than another set of multiple-choice exercises: repetition with feedback.

The price is the main reason to pause. At roughly $19.99/month for Standard or $29.99/month for Unlimited in the U.S. App Store examples I checked, Langua is closer to a serious learning tool than a casual app purchase. The annual plans reduce the monthly equivalent, but you should only pay annually if you know you will use it consistently.

My recommendation is to test it with one clear question: after two or three sessions, are you speaking more than you would without it? If the answer is yes, Langua may be worth paying for. If you mostly read transcripts, avoid the microphone, or feel annoyed by voice recognition, the value drops quickly.

Final Verdict

Langua is one of the most convincing AI language apps I have tested from a product perspective because it focuses on a real learner pain point: speaking. It does not just add an AI chatbot as a decoration. The conversation practice, Call Mode, corrections, transcripts, vocabulary saving, and review tools all point toward the same goal.

I would recommend Langua most strongly to intermediate beginners and intermediate learners who need more speaking practice than traditional apps provide. I would be more cautious for absolute beginners, budget-conscious learners, or anyone who wants a complete curriculum in one place.
