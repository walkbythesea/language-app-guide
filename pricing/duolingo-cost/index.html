How Much Does Duolingo Cost in 2026? Free, Super & Max Prices Compared

By Elena | Updated September 6, 2026

Duolingo is free to use. On my iOS account in September 2026, there are three paid options: Lite ($35.99/year), Super Individual ($16.99/month or $119.99/year), and Super Family ($143.99/year). Max no longer appears as a new subscription option on my account.

Prices and available plans may vary by account, platform, and country.

Duolingo Free Version: Limitations & What You Actually Get

Yes, Duolingo is free to download and use. You can access the full course in any language without paying. That hasn't changed.

What has changed — and this is the big story of 2025–2026 — is how much the free experience has been squeezed by the new energy system.

The Energy System: What Free Users Need to Know

In 2025, Duolingo began replacing the old Hearts system with a new Energy mechanic on mobile. The exact rules are still not identical for every account, but the basic idea is the same:

  • You receive a limited supply of Energy
  • Every question costs 1 energy — whether you answer correctly or not
  • When you run out, you either wait for it to recharge, watch an ad, or spend gems
  • Super and Max subscribers get unlimited energy

Under the old Hearts system, you'd only lose hearts when you made mistakes. With Energy, even correct answers can use your supply. In practice, this puts a predictable limit on how long many free users can study before they have to wait, watch an ad, or spend gems. The amount of Energy and recharge behavior may differ because Duolingo continues to test the system.

"With the energy system, I can imagine that a 2-hour session on Duolingo nowadays would be 50/50 learning and ads — which is exactly why they introduced it, to make Super Duolingo more appealing."

— a free-tier Duolingo user

The energy system is still rolling out gradually. Some accounts still have the old Hearts system, and the desktop web version currently doesn't use energy at all. This could change.

If Energy is your only frustration, the new Lite option may be the most relevant alternative; if both Energy and ads bother you, Super is the cleaner upgrade.

Duolingo Lite: A New Lower-Cost Plan Being Tested

Duolingo is testing a lower-cost subscription called Lite or Super Lite. It appeared on my iOS account alongside Individual and Family plans at $35.99/year, advertised as the equivalent of $2.99/month.

There is no earlier Lite price to compare with: the plan was not present when this guide was first published in March 2026. The important change is the arrival of a new paid tier between Free and Super, rather than a price increase to an existing Lite subscription.

My account also offered a 7-day Lite trial. The benefits screen described two main upgrades over Free:

  • Twice the daily Energy
  • Twice as fast Energy recharge

Lite still includes ads and does not provide unlimited Energy. That makes it a middle option for learners who regularly hit the Energy limit but do not care enough about removing ads to pay for Super.

This is still a test, not a universal plan. Duolingo CEO Luis von Ahn described Super Lite as a lower-priced, ad-supported experiment during the company's Q2 2026 earnings call. He said its price and availability depend on geography and emphasized that the company had not decided whether it would become permanent.

My take: Lite makes sense only if Energy is the main thing stopping you. At $35.99/year it is much cheaper than Super, but paying for a subscription that still shows ads will be a difficult trade-off for some learners.

Video Call Is Moving from Max to Super

Video Call used to be one of the clearest reasons to pay for Max. That changed in 2026. On my account, a Video Call with Lily now appears directly on the learning path with a “Try free with Super” button.

This is not just an isolated label in the app. In its Q2 2026 shareholder letter, Duolingo said that most new Super subscribers already had access to Video Call and that it expected to extend access to existing Super subscribers later in the year.

The rollout is gradual, so Video Call may not appear for every account, platform, language, or existing subscriber yet. My screenshots confirm that it is available through Super on my account; they do not prove that every Individual Super subscription includes unlimited calls. The Family offer, discussed below, did explicitly list Unlimited Video Call.

Super is worth it if you:

  • Use Duolingo daily on mobile
  • Find the energy limits or ads frustrating
  • Want to do more than 2–3 lessons a day
  • Travel and want offline access
  • Can commit to the annual plan for the best price

Super probably isn't worth it if you:

  • Only use Duolingo casually (a few times a week)
  • Primarily use the desktop web version
  • Are comfortable with the energy system's daily limits
  • Don't mind watching a few ads between lessons

What's Happening to Duolingo Max in 2026?

Max was originally Duolingo's highest-priced subscription and the only way to access AI features such as Video Call and Roleplay. That distinction is now much less clear.

Max did not appear anywhere on my current iOS purchase screen, while Video Call was offered with Super. The latest evidence suggests this is part of a phase-out for new subscriptions rather than a temporary display bug. Some existing Max subscribers still retain access, so the transition is not complete.

During Duolingo's Q2 2026 earnings call on August 5, CEO Luis von Ahn said the company had not yet decided Max's long-term role. He discussed keeping unlimited Video Call in Max, adding a different exclusive feature, or retiring the tier.

A more recent September 5 response in the Duolingo community said that Max was being phased out and that Roleplay was no longer included with new subscriptions. Duolingo has not published a separate global shutdown announcement, so I would describe Max as being phased out for new subscribers, not as already removed from every existing account.

If Max still appears for you, compare its exact features with Super before paying more—especially now that Video Call is moving down to Super. Existing Max customers may continue to see features or renewal options that new subscribers cannot buy.

When this guide was first published, I had recorded $119.99/year as the normal Family price. My latest account screen shows a regular price of $143.99/year—a $24 increase, or about 20%. The Back to School promotion briefly brought the first-year price back down to $119.99, but that did not make $119.99 the new permanent price.

How it works:

  • One person purchases the Family Plan and becomes the plan manager
  • The manager invites up to 5 additional members via email
  • Each member gets their own independent account with full Super features
  • Members do not need to live in the same household or be actual family members
  • Everyone keeps their own streaks, progress, and language selections

Duolingo's official Family Plan guide confirms that a Family plan covers six separate accounts and that members do not have to be relatives or live in the same household.

Tip: At the regular $143.99 price shown on my account, two people would effectively pay about $72 each, while six people would pay about $24 each for a year. It remains the cheapest legitimate way to get full Super benefits if you have a group you trust.

The 2026 “80% Off” Family Sale Explained

In 2026, I was shown a short Back to School offer that reduced Super Family from $143.99 to $119.99/year. The screen said the offer would end in five days, and it has now ended.

Although the button said “Get 80% Off,” the Family subscription itself was not reduced by 80%. The actual checkout-price reduction was $24:

  • Regular Family price: $143.99/year
  • Sale price: $119.99/year
  • Actual reduction from the regular Family price: approximately 16.7%

The small print explained the 80% figure: Duolingo was comparing the cost of one Family plan with the cost of purchasing six separate Individual Super subscriptions. At the regular prices shown on my account, six $119.99 Individual subscriptions would cost $719.94, while one $143.99 Family plan is about 80% cheaper than that total. It is a real per-person saving when all six places are used, but it is not the same as taking 80% off the Family checkout price.

The offer screen also listed Unlimited Energy, Unlimited Video Call, no ads, and access for the plan manager plus five other members. It warned that the subscription would renew automatically at the full price unless cancelled.

The promotion is useful to know about even though it has ended. It shows that Family discounts do happen, and it tells us what to check next time: the actual checkout price, the comparison behind the advertised percentage, and the renewal price.

Promotional Pricing

Duolingo occasionally offers discounted rates during events such as New Year and Back to School promotions. The 2026 Family offer I received reduced the first-year price from $143.99 to $119.99, but it lasted only a few days and renewed at the normal price.

These offers may not appear for every account. Before accepting one, check:

  • The amount charged today
  • The normal renewal price
  • Whether the advertised percentage refers to a checkout discount or per-person savings
  • Whether you are committing to annual billing

Duolingo also published the 2026 Family campaign through Google Play as “6 Super accounts for the price of 1,” with savings of up to 80% per person. The Google Play promotion page now shows the event as expired.

Desktop Web Workaround

This isn't a discount, but it's a legitimate way to avoid paying. The desktop web version of Duolingo currently does not use the energy system — meaning you can do unlimited lessons for free on your computer. If the energy limits on mobile are your main frustration, switching to desktop might solve the problem without spending anything. Keep in mind that Duolingo could change this at any time.

Duolingo Pricing by Country

Duolingo prices can be very different depending on where and how you subscribe. Even two people in the same country may see different screens while the company tests new plans and purchase flows.

Before comparing your price with someone else's, check:

  • Your country and billing currency
  • Whether you are subscribing through iOS, Android, or the website
  • Whether the price is monthly or annual
  • Whether tax is included
  • Whether you are seeing a trial, introductory offer, or retention offer
  • Whether Lite, Max, or another test is available on your account

Duolingo says it continuously optimizes its purchase flows, packaging, and pricing, and the company is known for extensive A/B testing. You can read more in its official company strategy overview. For that reason, the checkout screen on your own account is the only reliable source for the exact amount you will pay.

Choose Free If...

  • You're just trying Duolingo out to see if you like it
  • You only do a lesson or two a day and don't mind the energy system
  • You primarily use the desktop web version
  • You're on a tight budget and can tolerate ads
  • You use Duolingo as a supplement to other learning methods

Choose Super If...

  • You use Duolingo daily on your phone
  • The energy limits are cutting into your learning time
  • You want an uninterrupted, ad-free experience
  • You travel and want offline access to lessons
  • You want Video Call and it is included on your account
  • You can commit to the annual plan ($119.99/year on my account)

Choose Lite If...

  • You regularly run out of Energy
  • You want a cheaper option than Super
  • You are comfortable continuing to see ads
  • Lite is available on your account

Consider Max Only If...

  • Max appears on your account
  • It includes AI features that your Super offer does not
  • You have compared the current feature lists rather than assuming Video Call is Max-only
  • The additional features justify the exact price shown to you

Choose the Family Plan If...

  • You have at least one other person who uses Duolingo
  • You want the best per-person price available
  • You can organize a small group of friends, family, or classmates

Consider Alternatives If...

  • You want more structured grammar instruction (consider Babbel)
  • You need real human conversation practice (consider a tutor or italki)
  • You're an advanced learner who has outgrown Duolingo's content
  • You want in-depth cultural context alongside language learning
