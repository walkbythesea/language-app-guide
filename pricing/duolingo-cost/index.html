How Much Does Duolingo Cost in 2026? Free, Super & Max Prices Compared

By Elena | March 20, 2026

Duolingo has three tiers: Free ($0), Super ($95.99/year), and Max ($167.99/year). There's also a Family Plan starting at $119.99/year for up to 6 people.

Those numbers are simple enough, but the real question is messier. Duolingo doesn't publish pricing on their website, costs vary by country, and the free experience has gotten noticeably tighter since the energy system rolled out in 2025. As someone who has used both the free and paid versions — and who hears from readers every week asking is it actually worth paying for? — I'll walk you through every plan, what's changed recently, and help you figure out whether upgrading makes sense for your situation.

Is Duolingo Free? What You Actually Get Without Paying

Yes, Duolingo is free to download and use. You can access the full course in any language without paying. That hasn't changed.

What has changed — and this is the big story of 2025–2026 — is how much the free experience has been squeezed by the new energy system.

The Energy System: What Free Users Need to Know

In mid-2025, Duolingo began replacing the old Hearts system with a new Energy mechanic on mobile. Here's how it works:

  • You start each day with 25 energy units
  • Every question costs 1 energy — whether you answer correctly or not
  • When you run out, you either wait for it to recharge, watch an ad, or spend gems
  • Super and Max subscribers get unlimited energy

Under the old Hearts system, you'd only lose hearts when you made mistakes. A skilled learner could theoretically do unlimited lessons for free. With Energy, even a perfect run of 25 questions drains your daily supply. In practice, most free users get about 2–3 lessons per day on mobile before hitting the wall — roughly 15–20 minutes of learning time.

"With the energy system, I can imagine that a 2-hour session on Duolingo nowadays would be 50/50 learning and ads — which is exactly why they introduced it, to make Super Duolingo more appealing."

— a free-tier Duolingo user

The energy system is still rolling out gradually. Some accounts still have the old Hearts system, and the desktop web version currently doesn't use energy at all. This could change.

What Free Duolingo Includes

  • Full access to all language courses (40+ languages)
  • Core lesson content and exercises
  • Leaderboards and streak tracking
  • Stories (for supported languages)
  • Explain My Answer — AI-powered grammar explanations (this used to be a Max-only feature but became free for all users in January 2026)
  • Basic progress tracking

What Free Duolingo Does NOT Include

  • Unlimited energy (mobile)
  • Ad-free experience
  • Offline lesson downloads
  • Unlimited "Legendary" challenges
  • Streak repair (free users can use gems, but options are limited)
  • AI conversation features (Video Call, Roleplay)

My honest take: the free version of Duolingo is still genuinely useful — especially on desktop where the energy system doesn't apply. But on mobile, it now feels more like a freemium trial than a truly free product. For casual learners who are happy with a short daily session, it works fine. For anyone wanting to do more than 15–20 minutes a day, the energy wall will become a real frustration.

"If you can easily afford it, you might as well go for it. But on a pension, every penny counts, so I'll stick with the free version. It's not like I can afford to travel to practice the language anyway — it just keeps my brain active."

— a retired learner

Is Super Duolingo Worth It?

"My Super Duolingo just renewed for the second time and it's absolutely worth it. I pay $62 after tax once a year. I'm on a 693-day streak at this point and I have learned so much."

— a 693-day streak holder

"For me it's worth it to save the 30-45 seconds after each lesson of ad time. I could start another lesson instead — the time adds up over the year."

Super is worth it if you:

  • Use Duolingo daily on mobile
  • Find the energy limits or ads frustrating
  • Want to do more than 2–3 lessons a day
  • Travel and want offline access
  • Can commit to the annual plan for the best price

Super probably isn't worth it if you:

  • Only use Duolingo casually (a few times a week)
  • Primarily use the desktop web version
  • Are comfortable with the energy system's daily limits
  • Don't mind watching a few ads between lessons

Duolingo Max: Cost & AI Features

Billing Cycle Price Monthly Cost
Monthly $29.99/month $29.99
Annual $167.99/year ~$14.00

Duolingo Max includes everything in Super, plus two AI-powered features:

  • Video Call — A simulated video conversation with Duolingo characters. You speak into your microphone and the character responds in real time, adapting to your level. It's the closest thing Duolingo offers to real speaking practice.
  • Roleplay — Text-based scenario practice where you navigate conversations in your target language (ordering food, checking into a hotel, etc.). The AI adapts to your responses and provides corrections.

One important note: Explain My Answer, which used to be a Max-exclusive feature, became free for all users in January 2026. This was one of Max's most useful features, so its removal from the paid tier narrows the gap between Super and Max.

Max is currently available for Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, and Korean (learning from English). Availability may expand over time, but if your target language isn't on this list, Max offers no benefit over Super.

At roughly double the price of Super, Max is a harder sell. The AI features are genuinely interesting, but they're not a replacement for real conversation practice. If you're already supplementing Duolingo with a tutor, language exchange partner, or another app focused on speaking, the extra cost may not be justified.

Duolingo Family Plan: Best Value for Groups

Plan Annual Price Max Members Per Person/Year Per Person/Month
Super Family $119.99 6 ~$20.00 ~$1.67
Max Family $239.99 6 ~$40.00 ~$3.33

How it works:

  • One person purchases the Family Plan and becomes the plan manager
  • The manager invites up to 5 additional members via email
  • Each member gets their own independent account with full plan features
  • Members do not need to live in the same household or be actual family members
  • Everyone keeps their own streaks, progress, and language selections

Tip: If you can find even one other person to split Super Family with, you're both paying $60/year instead of $96 — and with six people, it drops to just $20/year each. This is by far the cheapest way to get Super Duolingo.

Ways to Save on Duolingo

Free Trial

Duolingo typically offers a 14-day free trial of Super Duolingo (sometimes 7 days depending on your region and account). This is the easiest way to test whether the paid features are worth it for you. Just remember to set a reminder to cancel before the trial ends if you decide it's not worth continuing.

Student Discount (~50% Off)

Students at accredited institutions in the US, Canada, UK, Ireland, Australia, and New Zealand can get approximately 50% off Super Duolingo's annual plan — bringing it down to around $47.99/year (~$4.00/month). Verification is handled through SheerID, Student Beans, or UNiDAYS depending on your region.

Annual vs. Monthly

This is the simplest way to save. Paying annually instead of monthly cuts your effective cost significantly:

Plan Monthly Cost (Monthly Billing) Monthly Cost (Annual Billing) Savings
Super $13.99 $7.99 43%
Max $29.99 ~$14.00 53%

Promotional Pricing

Duolingo occasionally offers discounted rates during events like Black Friday, back-to-school season, or New Year promotions. These are unpredictable and not guaranteed, but if your renewal is coming up, it can be worth waiting a few weeks to see if a deal appears. Some users also report receiving retention offers after cancelling their subscription.

Desktop Web Workaround

This isn't a discount, but it's a legitimate way to avoid paying. The desktop web version of Duolingo currently does not use the energy system — meaning you can do unlimited lessons for free on your computer. If the energy limits on mobile are your main frustration, switching to desktop might solve the problem without spending anything. Keep in mind that Duolingo could change this at any time.

Which Duolingo Plan Should You Choose?

"I think it depends on your goals. If you want to use Duo for a few minutes a day to learn a few words, the free version is more than enough. If you want to really bootstrap learning to read, listen to, write and speak a language, you need at least an hour a day, which would be nearly impossible with the free version."

— an experienced Duolingo user

Choose Free If...

  • You're just trying Duolingo out to see if you like it
  • You only do a lesson or two a day and don't mind the energy system
  • You primarily use the desktop web version
  • You're on a tight budget and can tolerate ads
  • You use Duolingo as a supplement to other learning methods

Choose Super If...

  • You use Duolingo daily on your phone
  • The energy limits are cutting into your learning time
  • You want an uninterrupted, ad-free experience
  • You travel and want offline access to lessons
  • You can commit to the annual plan ($95.99/year)

Choose Max If...

  • You want AI-powered speaking practice (Video Call)
  • Your target language is supported by Max features
  • You don't have other options for conversation practice
  • You're comfortable paying roughly double the Super price

Choose the Family Plan If...

  • You have at least one other person who uses Duolingo
  • You want the best per-person price available
  • You can organize a small group of friends, family, or classmates

Consider Alternatives If...

  • You want more structured grammar instruction (consider Babbel)
  • You need real human conversation practice (consider a tutor or italki)
  • You're an advanced learner who has outgrown Duolingo's content
  • You want in-depth cultural context alongside language learning

"I'm really excited about maintaining my streak, and honestly, I enjoy spending time here much more than on social media. It has helped me move away from endless doom-scrolling and focus on something meaningful. It truly feels like I'm investing in myself."

— a daily Duolingo learner

Whether you spend $0 or $96 a year, the fact that you're learning a language instead of scrolling is already a win. The right plan is the one that keeps you coming back.
